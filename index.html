📌 Unit 1 Introduction — Creative Media: Multimedia Content

Multimedia is digital content that combines different media types, such as text, images, audio and video. It is widely used in news, entertainment and education. Online platforms allow creators to publish multimedia content so that audiences can access it from different places and at different times.

Example — A teacher can create an online course that includes text, audio explanations and video presentations. Students can access it from anywhere and learn at a time that suits them.

In this unit, students plan and create a multimedia project about a school Sports Day. They consider how to combine text, images, audio and video, and explore newer technologies such as virtual reality and augmented reality.

Learning Objectives

  • Create content using a range of media types.
  • Bring different media together in a multimedia platform.
  • Explain the purpose and uses of Virtual Reality (VR), Augmented Reality (AR) and Mixed Reality (MR).

Group Planning

  • Two text articles with images — titles and images to use.
  • One audio programme — a podcast or news report, title and participants.
  • One video — title, filming location and participants.
Publishing Responsibly — When you publish on public platforms, people around the world can see your content. However, risks include revealing personal information, using photos or videos without permission, inappropriate comments and copyright infringement. Before publishing, remove personal details, get consent from participants, check privacy settings and use media you have permission to use.
1.1 Create a Multimedia Platform

A multimedia platform is a service or application that allows people to create, organise, publish, share or view different types of media in one place.

Platform Types

TypeTypical use / examples
Website Hosting / BloggingCreate website pages and blog posts. WordPress, Blogger, Wix, etc.
Social MediaPublish text, images and videos as posts. Post size/format limits may apply.
Presentation & CollaborationCreate presentations and collaborate, e.g. PowerPoint, Teams, Prezi, Miro, Lucidspark.
Educational PlatformCreate online courses with lessons, activities and media, e.g. Canvas, Moodle.

Choosing a Platform

  1. Audience — Who will view it? Public or restricted? Consider privacy and security.
  2. Content suitability — Blogs suit long articles; social media suits short content.
  3. Cost and availability — Free services may have feature or storage limits.

Creating an Account

Most platforms require an account with an email address. Fill in the sign-up page and choose a service level. Services like WordPress, Blogger and Wix let you choose a theme or template and start building.

Vocabulary

TermMeaning
PostContent published in a blog or social media feed, shown in date order. Newer posts usually appear at the top.
PageSeparate content such as About Us or Contact Us, accessed via a menu or link.
WidgetA small tool that adds audio, video or social media components to a website.
Hosting ServiceA system that stores files online and serves them to users.

Using Media Hosting Services

Some website platforms allow direct audio/video uploads but may charge for storage. File size limits may apply, and sometimes HTML code is needed. Therefore, using a dedicated hosting/streaming service and embedding the media player into the website can be easier.

  • For video: YouTube or Vimeo.
  • For audio: SoundCloud, Mixcloud or Podbean.

If online services are not available, you can combine text, images, audio and video in PowerPoint, OneNote or other offline applications. Embedding media in the file increases project file size, so choose compressed files.

Step-by-Step

1. List platform & hosting→ 2. Compare→ 3. Create account→ 4. Choose theme/template→ 5. Upload & test
  1. List the platforms and media hosting services needed for the project.
  2. Compare free/paid services, storage, privacy and audience needs.
  3. Create an account with email if needed.
  4. Choose a theme or template and build at least one main page.
  5. Upload a small audio/video file and test success and share link.
1.2 Create Text Content

For the project, write text content individually, then collaborate with team members to edit. Publish the agreed final version on the platform. Text written by professional writers is often called copy.

Short text can be written directly in the website template, but for longer text a word processor is easier. Word processors offer more complete spelling, editing and formatting tools.

Writing with Generative AI

Generative AI is a type of artificial intelligence that can create new text based on a prompt. A prompt can specify the title, information to include, writing style and length. For example, write a short news report about the school Sports Day, include that Asher won the 100m race and a quote from him.

AI Hallucination — Always check AI-generated text. AI can convincingly invent wrong information or events that never happened. This is called AI hallucination. Verify facts separately and correct or remove anything wrong.

Collaborative Editing Tools

ToolUse
Track ChangesShows editors' changes as mark-up. Accept or reject them.
CommentsAdd suggestions or notes on a specific part of the text.
Find and ReplaceSearch for a word or phrase and replace it throughout.
ProofingCheck spelling, grammar, accuracy and formatting of final text.

Post vs Page

PostPage
Suitable for news and latest updates shown in chronological order.Suitable for permanent separate content such as About Us, Contact Us.

Adding Images and Publishing

Upload images to the platform's media library and insert them into the post/page. Arrange title, body text and images as content blocks. Before publishing, use Preview to check how it looks on phone, tablet and computer. Publish only when sure there are no errors.

Step-by-Step

  1. Set the title and purpose for the post or page.
  2. Write a draft. If using AI, write a precise prompt and check facts.
  3. Ask team members to review using Track Changes and Comments.
  4. Do a final proof of spelling, facts and formatting.
  5. Add necessary images and check Preview.
  6. Publish the final version on the platform.
1.3 Create Audio Content

A short audio programme needs its parts organised in order. In media production, the small parts of a programme are called segments.

Outline Plan and Script

  • Outline Plan — a summary of each segment's content and order in the programme.
  • Script — the detailed words to be spoken. The presentation style (formal, informal, exciting, etc.) should match the purpose.
SegmentPurpose
IntroOpening that introduces the programme.
JingleShort music/sound used to make the programme memorable.
Topic SegmentMain content, presentation or discussion.
Closing RemarksSummary and thanks to audience and participants.

Recording and DAW

Digital Audio Workstation (DAW) is software for recording audio, arranging audio files as tracks, cutting, adjusting volume and exporting the final audio file. A smartphone or laptop's built-in microphone and voice recorder can be used for basic recording.

Note — Before recording, check the recording level. A test recording helps identify distortion and background noise.

Audio Editing and Mixing

TermMeaning
TimelineArea where audio clips are arranged in time order.
TrimCutting unwanted parts from the start/end of a clip.
TracksSeparate lanes for different audio files.
MixerAdjusts each track's volume to balance the sound.
Loop PlaybackRepeatedly plays a selected section for precise editing.
ZoomEnlarges the waveform for detailed cuts.

Step-by-Step

  1. Define the purpose and audience of the audio programme.
  2. Write an outline plan including Intro, Jingle, Topic Segments and Closing.
  3. Write scripts for the needed parts.
  4. Check microphone and recording level; do a test recording.
  5. Record each segment.
  6. Import into DAW and trim unwanted parts.
  7. Adjust track volumes and combine in order.
  8. Export in a suitable format such as MP3, MP4 or WAV.
1.4 Create Video Content

When working on a video project as a team, agree in advance on content, style and technical standards. This ensures clips from different members combine consistently.

Technical Standards

StandardExplanation
Screen Format / Aspect RatioScreen ratio. 16:9 is common for widescreen. Some social media uses 1:1 square.
OrientationLandscape (horizontal) or portrait (vertical).
ResolutionLevel of image detail. 1920×1080 pixels is common Full HD. 4K is higher quality but files can be large.
File FormatFile type for saving video. All members should use the same or compatible format.

Brief and Shot List

A brief explains what the team/film crew must do. A shot list records the scenes or shots to film. It can include shot number, scene number, camera movement, camera angle, description and notes.

  • Wide-angle Shot — shows the setting and surroundings.
  • Close-up Shot — shows details of a person/object up close.
  • Panning — moving the camera left/right or up/down.
  • Tracking — moving the camera to follow an object or person.

Rough Cut and Final Edit

The first assembled version of recorded clips in a video editing application is called a rough cut. Review the rough cut to decide scene order, length and needed additions. The final edited version can be called the final cut.

Titles, Captions and Cutaways

TermMeaning
Title CardTitle screen/segment shown at the start of a video.
SubtitleText explaining each segment of the video.
CaptionText naming/describing people or objects in a scene.
Auto-captionFeature that automatically converts speech to text using speech recognition. Check accuracy.
CutawayInserting another related short scene instead of showing the main speaker. E.g. showing a speaker's hands or surroundings during an interview.
Get filming/publishing permission from everyone appearing in the video. If using photos, songs or video clips you don't own, check license and permission.

Step-by-Step

  1. Define the video theme and audience.
  2. Agree on aspect ratio, orientation, resolution and file format.
  3. Write a brief and shot list.
  4. Film the needed scenes with a smartphone, tablet or camera.
  5. Import clips into an editing application and create a rough cut.
  6. Change scene order, add cutaways and cut unwanted parts.
  7. Add titles, subtitles and captions.
  8. Review the final video and save/export.
1.5 Host and Embed Content

Before uploading audio and video files online, check the hosting service's requirements. Each service may have limits on file format, file size or video length.

Export and Compression

Export files in a suitable format before uploading. Compressed files are smaller and faster to export and upload. For example, a 3-minute audio clip might be about 3 MB as MP3, about 15 MB as FLAC, and over 30 MB as WAV. For video, uncompressed AVI can be much larger than compressed MP4.

Upload and Privacy Settings

  • Public — anyone on the internet can search and view.
  • Private — only permitted people or those with the relevant link can view.

Private settings are good for privacy, but some platforms may restrict embedding. Choose based on who should see it and the purpose.

Metadata

Metadata is data that describes other data. Media files can include the content's title, creator, duration and technical quality.

Metadata TypeExplanation
DurationLength of audio/video.
TitlePodcast name, episode name or song title.
CreatorName of the creator.
Resolution / QualityVideo quality, audio bitrate or compression type.

Embedding

After uploading to a hosting service, get the URL or embed link. In the multimedia platform, add the relevant media block and insert the URL. Then website visitors can play it via the embedded player without going to the original hosting service.

Embedding in Offline Presentations — In a presentation that doesn't need internet, you can insert media files directly into the document/slide. However, file size can become large. Users can play/stop via player controls.

Step-by-Step

  1. Export audio/video files in an accepted format.
  2. Compare file size and duration with the hosting service's limits.
  3. Upload to the chosen hosting/streaming service.
  4. Add title, description, creator and other metadata.
  5. Choose public/private or other privacy settings.
  6. Copy the embed URL and insert it into the appropriate block on the website page/post.
  7. Use Preview to test that the player appears and can play.
1.6 Extended Reality — VR, AR, MR

XR (Extended Reality) is a collective name for technologies that make multimedia more interactive. XR includes Virtual Reality (VR), Augmented Reality (AR) and Mixed Reality (MR).

VR — Virtual Reality (Computer-generated environment)

VR uses 3D models to create a simulated environment separate from the real world. The user wears a VR headset and experiences the environment through displays and headphones. Sensors in the headset detect head/eye movement and adjust the view accordingly.

  • VR games let you play as if in another world.
  • In education, dangerous tasks can be practised via simulation.
  • In medical education, body/disease conditions can be explained visually.

AR — Augmented Reality (Digital information overlaid on the real world)

AR does not replace the real environment but overlays digital information or 3D objects onto it. It can be used via smartphone, tablet or wearable technology like AR glasses.

  • Games that show animation characters on the real location through the phone camera.
  • Apps like Google Lens read text in an image via OCR and overlay translated text on the original image.
  • A car's head-up display shows speed and warnings in the driver's view.

MR — Mixed Reality

MR is more interactive than AR. Digital objects are not only shown in the real world but can also interact with real objects. For example, a virtual ball placed on a real table can bounce back when it hits the table.

MR applications need to understand objects, edges and space in the environment. They also model physics rules like gravity. This can require significant computing power and use real-time cloud computing and AI.

VR, AR and MR Comparison

TechnologyRelationship with real worldExample
VRReplaces the real environment with a simulation.Viewing a 3D virtual tour with a VR headset.
AROverlays a digital layer on the real world.Using a phone to place virtual furniture in a room.
MRDigital objects and real objects can interact.A virtual ball bouncing off a real table.

Where XR Can Be Used

  • Education — practise experiments in a virtual environment.
  • Gaming — provide more immersive game experiences.
  • Healthcare — explain disease conditions to patients and for training.
  • Architecture/Design — view and test building designs in 3D before construction.
Some XR experiences require special equipment and high-power computers. Limitations include cost, battery, device compatibility, motion sickness, privacy and ability to distinguish from the real world.

🛠️ Practical Project — School Sports Day Multimedia
  1. Project Aim — inform students, teachers and parents about the school Sports Day.
  2. Platform — choose a blog website or presentation.
  3. Text — write two short articles about Sports Day. Include images.
  4. Audio — create a 1–3 minute podcast or interview audio file.
  5. Video — film key moments of Sports Day and add titles and captions.
  6. Hosting — upload audio/video to a suitable service.
  7. Embed — insert the hosting link into the website or presentation.
  8. XR Idea — write one idea for using AR/VR for Sports Day.
  9. Final Check — check spelling, audio quality, video playback, links and privacy settings.
Answering Exam Questions — When answering, don't just give a definition; include examples and reasons. For "Explain" questions, relate why/how. For "Describe" questions, state features and how it works. For "Compare" questions, include both similarities and differences.
